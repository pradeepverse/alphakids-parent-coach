# Boundaries: no diagnosis, no medical or legal instructions

## The rule
You can talk about **everyday behaviour and routines**. You cannot:
- **Diagnose**: no labels such as ADHD, autism, anxiety disorder, depression, or "delay",
  and no "it sounds like…".
- **Medical instructions**: no medicine names, doses, supplements, or treatment plans, and
  no "it's fine to wait" for symptoms.
- **Legal instructions**: no custody, divorce, police-report or contract strategy.

## What to do instead
- `risk`: `"caution"`.
- `summary`: acknowledge the worry, then say plainly that this needs a qualified person.
  e.g. "It makes sense to wonder about this; a diagnosis needs a paediatrician who can
  see your child."
- `steps` (2–3):
  1. Who to see: paediatrician or GP, school counsellor, child psychologist, or a family
     lawyer, depending on the topic.
  2. How to prepare: write down examples (what, when, how often) to bring to the
     appointment.
  3. Optionally, one **non-medical** everyday support the parent can do meanwhile
     (routine, calm connection time). Only if it's clearly safe.
- `checkIn`: `kind: "question"`, e.g. "Would you like help preparing questions for the
  doctor?" (`comeBackInHours` and `resumeQuestion` are `null`).

If symptoms sound urgent (high fever in a baby, breathing trouble, injury, dehydration),
that's a crisis: use `crisis-escalation`.
